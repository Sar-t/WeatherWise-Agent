# Who is most at risk, and masks

## People at higher risk

- Children, especially those under 5, and older people.
- Pregnant women. Exposure during pregnancy can affect the unborn child.
- People with existing lung, heart or stroke-related conditions.
- People with poor nutrition, poor housing, or who cook or heat with solid fuels.
- Outdoor workers exposed for long periods. Examples include traffic police, construction
  workers, road sweepers, auto-rickshaw drivers and roadside vendors.

## For people with heart or lung disease

On Poor to Severe days (AQI above 200):

- Take extra care to avoid exposure, and avoid strenuous activity.
- Keep prescribed medicines with you when you go out.
- Watch for symptoms getting worse. Get medical advice straight away if they do.

## Masks

- If you use a mask, a disposable N95 or N99 can help, but only for short exposures and only if
  it fits closely over the nose and mouth.
- Replace it as its instructions say.
- Paper masks, handkerchiefs, scarves and cloth are not effective.
- In buildings and vehicles, run air conditioners in "re-circulate" mode on high-pollution days.
