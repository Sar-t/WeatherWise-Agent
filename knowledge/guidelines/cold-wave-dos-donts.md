# Cold wave: staying safe

## Before a cold wave

- Follow the local weather forecast.
- Keep enough winter clothing. Several layers work better than one.
- Keep emergency supplies ready.

## During a cold wave

- Stay indoors as much as you can, and cut down on travel to avoid cold wind.
- Wear several loose, light, windproof warm layers rather than one heavy layer. Tight clothing
  reduces blood circulation.
- Keep dry. If you get wet, cover your head, neck, hands and toes well, because most body heat is
  lost there.
- Mittens are warmer than gloves. Wear a hat, a muffler, and insulated, waterproof shoes.
- Drink hot fluids regularly, and eat healthy food, including fruit and vegetables rich in
  vitamin C.
- Check on older people, children, and neighbours who live alone.
- Don't drink alcohol. It lowers body temperature and raises the risk of hypothermia.

## Don't ignore shivering

Shivering is the first sign that the body is losing heat. Get indoors.

## Heating safely

- Keep rooms ventilated when using heaters, so you don't breathe in toxic fumes.
- Take safety measures with electric and gas heaters. Turn them off when they aren't needed, and
  have them serviced regularly.
- Never leave heating pots or open fires burning unattended, or in a closed room. They can start
  fires, and they release carbon monoxide; lives have been lost to it in closed rooms.
- Carbon monoxide has no colour, smell or taste. Signs of poisoning include headache, dizziness,
  weakness, nausea and drowsiness. Never sleep in a vehicle with the engine running.
- If you suspect poisoning, move to fresh air at once and get medical help quickly.
