# Particulate matter and health

## What it is

Particulate matter (PM) is the most common indicator of air pollution, and its effects on health
are well established. It is a mixture of tiny particles, mainly:

- sulfates and nitrates
- ammonia
- sodium chloride
- black carbon
- mineral dust
- water

## Why it matters

The World Health Organization (WHO) links fine-particle pollution to several diseases:

- heart disease and stroke
- chronic lung disease
- lower respiratory infections
- lung cancer

Outdoor air pollution causes millions of early deaths each year. Most of them happen in low- and
middle-income countries, and the burden is highest in the South-East Asia and Western Pacific
regions. WHO also names air pollution as one of the greatest environmental risks to children's
health.

## Other common pollutants

- **Ozone:** at ground level, it forms from other gases in sunlight and is a main part of smog.
- **Nitrogen dioxide (NO2):** comes mostly from vehicles and industry.
- **Sulfur dioxide (SO2):** comes from burning coal and oil.
- **Carbon monoxide (CO):** a toxic gas from incomplete burning of fuels.
