# Sun protection

Sun protection is recommended whenever the UV index is 3 or above.

## Protecting yourself

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends:

- Limit time in the midday sun.
- Seek shade.
- Wear protective clothing.
- Wear a broad-brimmed hat to protect your eyes, face, ears and neck.
- Wear wraparound sunglasses that block 99-100% of UV-A and UV-B.
- Use broad-spectrum sunscreen on skin that clothes can't cover.

Shade and clothing protect better than sunscreen. Sunscreen should not be used as a reason to
stay longer in the sun.

## Children and eyes

- Children and teenagers are especially vulnerable. Sunburn in childhood raises the risk of skin
  cancer later in life, and more UV can reach a child's retina.
- Darker-skinned people burn less, but they can still develop skin cancer.
- Eye protection matters for everyone. UV can inflame the eye in the short term, and long
  exposure contributes to cataracts.
