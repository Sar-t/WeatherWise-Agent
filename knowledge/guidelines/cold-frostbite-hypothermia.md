# Frostbite and hypothermia

## Frostbite

- **Signs:** numbness, and white or pale fingers, toes, earlobes or nose tip. With longer
  exposure, the skin can turn pale, hard and numb, with black blisters.
- **What to do:** see a doctor straight away. Warm the affected area in warm water, not hot water:
  it should feel comfortable to unaffected skin.
- **Don't:** massage a frostbitten area. It can cause more damage.

## Hypothermia

Severe cold can lower body temperature. Signs include:

- shivering
- difficulty speaking
- sleepiness
- stiff muscles
- heavy breathing
- weakness, or loss of consciousness

Hypothermia is a medical emergency that needs immediate medical attention.

## While getting help for someone with hypothermia

- Move them somewhere warm and change them into dry clothes.
- Warm them with skin-to-skin contact and dry layers of blankets, clothes or towels.
- If they are fully alert, give warm drinks, never alcohol. Give no fluids if they are not fully
  alert.
- Seek medical help if they get worse.
