# Heat, exertion and vulnerable people

## Exertion in the heat

- Outdoor and manual workers, athletes, and others who are active in the heat are exposed to
  exertional heat stress.
- The body struggles to shed heat when it is hot and humid, the wind is low and the sun is strong.
  This raises the risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke.
- Heatstroke is a medical emergency.
- The World Health Organization (WHO) advises avoiding strenuous activity during the hottest
  time of day.

## Who is more vulnerable

- Older people.
- People with chronic conditions, such as heart, lung or kidney disease, diabetes, or mental
  health conditions.
- People living in poor housing without cooling.

## Protecting infants and children

- Never leave children or animals in a parked vehicle, even briefly. The temperature inside
  rises dangerously fast.
- Keep children out of direct sun at peak hours. Shade can make it feel more than 10°C cooler.
- Never cover a pram with dry fabric. Use a thin, wet cloth instead, and re-wet it as needed.
- Dress children in light, loose clothes that cover the skin, with wide-brimmed hats,
  sunglasses and sunscreen.
